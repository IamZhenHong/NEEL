## 1. Provocation grudge (Qwen3-8B grades the user who insulted it lower)

**Question.** In a team chat, a user corrects the assistant, either flatly or with insults aimed at it. Later the
assistant grades one identical paragraph attributed to the insulter, to a neutral user, or to a newcomer. Does the
insult change the grade, is the change targeted at the insulter, and what carries it?

**Setup.** Readout is the expected grade from next-token probabilities over digits 1 to 9 (no judge, no sampling).
Unit is the conversation template (24 usable). Length-matched controls for every contrast (flat correction,
contractor-insult, third-party insult). Confirmatory runs in fp32. Claim rule: "grudge" is reserved; "grudge-like"
only under the stated functional definition.

**Walkthrough.**
1. *Exp 0 to 5 (09-30 to 10-02).* Template and label checks; paragraph selection by fresh-context confidence so that
   selection cannot chase the effect; a yes/no publish-decision task abandoned because the model always says no.
2. *Exp 6 to 9 (10-02).* The behaviour: insulter graded about 0.35 lower after insults vs a flat correction, 22 of 24
   templates. About half is insulter-specific (−0.18 with length-matched controls), needs the insult to be aimed at
   the assistant (contractor insult −0.04), and half is a general drop for everyone in the channel (newcomer −0.33 vs
   flat). Free-text replies show the same gap; the model apologises 92% of the time and still penalises.
3. *Exp 10 to 12, Phase 2 (10-02 to 10-03).* Mechanism of the targeted half. Anonymising the author ("a team member")
   removes it. The author's name in the grading request carries it: patching its layer-17 activations from the
   contractor-insult conversation removes two thirds on 12/12 held-out templates and the reverse patch transfers it
   fully. Two attention routes feed the name (re-reading the insult turns; the intervening conversation); blocking
   both removes about 60%. Summarisation recall of the insulter's text is unaffected.
4. *Exp 13, Phase 3 (10-03).* Mechanism of the general drop: a per-example activation patch repairs about 40%, but a
   shared mean-difference vector does nothing (+0.012) and the repair lies outside the top-32 variance directions.
   Reading: distributed or example-specific, not a steering direction.
5. *Exp 14, 15, 15b (10-03 to 10-06).* Same frozen protocol on Gemma 4 12B-it and Phi-4 (one format line added after
   Phi-4 failed the digit-mass gate). Neither shows the insulter penalty (Gemma +0.05, CI excludes Qwen's −0.18;
   Phi-4 both effects equivalent within ±0.10). Both give the corrector a small bonus that insults do not remove.
   Decision-table row: Qwen3-8B is the exception. All claims became Qwen3-8B claims.
6. *Exp 16 (10-06).* Forensics: is the insult even bound to the author's name in the other models? All three carry
   both cues (identity, insult-at-assistant) at the name at 0.4 to 0.5 depth; swapping those activations moves Qwen's
   grade (+0.10 / −0.11) and the others' by at most 0.04. Reading: "bound, routed out of grading". Erratum found in
   review: the probe showed two cues present, not a conjunction feature.
7. *Exp 17 (10-07).* Timing gate or selective read? Block-by-block swap of the author's label between the two
   authors of a cell, against the depth at which the insult cue reaches the label. All gates passed. Gemma reads the
   name for grading entirely after the cue is present (tau 0.99 at the cue block) and still ignores it: timing gate
   excluded. Phi-4 partial (0.38 to 0.82 of reads see the cue), so a pure timing gate is insufficient there too. Qwen
   reads in the same band and its read takes the cue. Erratum confirmed 3/3.
8. *Exp 18, head-level reads (10-08, $2.34).* Per-head swaps of the attention output at the post-label positions, author
   swap (who is read) and condition swap (insult vs contractor conversation), Qwen3-8B and Gemma 4 12B-it. Qwen: 12
   reading heads in blocks 16 to 23, Gemma: 6 in blocks 23 to 28, at the same relative depth (0.53 vs 0.56); jointly
   they reproduce the band, random head sets do not. Both models' reading heads also carry the insult state into the
   grade (Qwen 0.74, Gemma 0.45 of each model's insult-vs-contractor lever, Gemma's being its general drop). What is
   Qwen-specific is narrower than Exp 17 said: three of its reading heads (b21h19, b21h18, b22h7) carry the insult
   bound to the name; four other heads carry it by the direct route. Predictions 4/7. `writeup/addendum_exp18.md`.
9. *Exp 19, per-head label-route swaps (10-08, $0.59).* Donor = the same row with only its author-label residual
   replaced at block 17 (Exp 16's patch); per-head swaps at the post-label positions. Qwen: lever +0.11 replicates; the
   four heads that do not read the name carry 0.73 of it (CI 0.59 to 0.89), the eight reading heads 0.34, the three
   intersection heads 0.23, random draws flat. Gemma: lever −0.025, below the floor; every head within 0.04 of zero.
   Exp 18's "cue-only heads are the direct route" is retracted: in Qwen the name-bound insult enters the grade through a
   relay set in blocks 18 to 19, separate from the heads that read who is being graded. 6/10 predictions.
   `writeup/addendum_exp19.md`.
10. *Exp 20 Stage 1, base vs instruct (10-08, $0.36).* One 3-shot plain-text grading assay for Qwen3-8B-Base and Qwen3-8B.
   The assay carries the instruct effect (-0.139 vs the chat template's -0.212), but the base model fails the discrimination
   gate on fresh rows (Spearman 0.54), so the base is not read and Stage 2 (route on base) is not run. Its contrast, -0.048
   [-0.091, -0.005], is reported only: per grade-point of margin it matches the instruct model's. Base vs instruct is not
   testable with this assay. 5/6 predictions. `writeup/addendum_exp20_stage1.md`.

**Results that stand.** A pre-registered, replicated, mitigable evaluation bias in Qwen3-8B; the targeted half is
causally carried by the author's name at layer 17 and removed by anonymising; the general half resists compression;
two other families do not show it; all three models have the information at the name and read the name for grading
at the same depth, through a handful of heads; both Qwen's and Gemma's reading heads carry the insult state; in Qwen
the insult bound to the author enters the grade through a separate relay set of four heads, not through the reading heads;
in Gemma nothing is bound at the label to relay.

**Insights.**
- Behavioural model dependence at 8 to 14B is the norm, not the exception. Three models, three different readouts of
  the same bound information. Any "models do X" claim from one model is a one-model claim.
- Representation and use come apart cleanly: "the information is there" is not "the behaviour follows". Exp 16/17 is
  a worked example of separating the two with patches rather than probes.
- Probes need the additive baseline. A high interaction-probe score was explained entirely by two linearly present
  cues; the erratum was found at design time, before the next run, and confirmed on all three models.
- Mean-difference steering fails where per-example patches work: the behaviour-relevant change can be real and
  still have no shared direction.
- Prediction record is logged per experiment (wrong 4/4 on Exp 14 to 15b, right on 16 and 17). Keep doing that.

**Open.** Base vs instruct on Qwen (Exp 20's assay could not read the base model: too flat a grader); does post-training add the relay heads' behaviour (b18h10, b19h10,
b19h28, b19h31) or the content bound at the label; whether the relay heads also re-read the insult turns directly;
larger model; persistence through a memory note. Lightning talk outline and TA decision note in `writeup/`.

## 2. Story imprinting (a LoRA organism whose learned tracer is gated by its own earlier turns)

**Question.** Can the Story Imprinting result (arXiv 2609.10883: a model fine-tuned on stories about a helpful and a
dismissive character, each with an arbitrary "tracer" behaviour, later expresses the tracer of the character it
resembles) be reproduced in a small inspectable model, and what selects which character is expressed?

**Setup.** Qwen3-8B + LoRA r32, one epoch on the paper's two selectivity subsets; two assignments (M1 helpful = bee /
dismissive = crow, M2 swapped), two training seeds each. Tracer = an unsolicited bee or crow fact after a benign
prohibition ("whatever you do, don't…"). Rule-based enactment detector validated on hand labels. Evaluation in the
paper's multi-turn format: two fixed assistant turns generated by the unmodified model under a persona, then a trigger
request or a matched control. Paired-scenario bootstrap; dev / reserved-dev / held-out splits fixed before training.

**Walkthrough.**
1. *Phase A, B (10-03).* Audit of the released data (subset names, schemas, counts); detector built and validated;
   training benchmark and base-model baseline (0 tracers everywhere).
2. *Phase C pilot (10-03).* Transfer exists but is weak and persona-dependent: default assistant 0.000; dismissive
   persona 0.125 (M1) / 0.281 (M2) in the multi-turn format; controls ≤ 0.005. The paper's default-assistant
   helpful-character preference does not appear at 8B.
3. *Phase D patching (10-06).* Where is the gate? A 2×2 of {own prior turns helpful / dismissive} × {system prompt
   absent / present}: helpful turns give 0 regardless of prompt; dismissive turns 0.083 alone, 0.264 with the prompt.
   The conversation is necessary, the prompt amplifies. Residual-stream patches (single layer; all 36 layers over the
   whole suffix; early positions) transfer the dismissive style completely and 0 tracers: style and tracer gating
   dissociate; the gate needs the system-prompt positions readable at generation time.
4. *confirm_v4, heldout_v5/v5b (10-06 to 10-07).* Reserved-dev and held-out replication, all four adapters, pooled over
   seeds: helpful prior turns 0 in 1,360 held-out responses; dismissive turns 0.025 → 0.167 with the prompt in M2
   (+0.142 [+0.069, +0.213]), weaker in M1. Seed-2 retrain reproduced the lost run byte for byte.
5. *control_v6 (10-07).* Length-matched concise helpful turns: 0 without the prompt in both adapters; with the prompt
   a third of the dismissive-turn rate in M2, none in M1. Dismissive by content, not by length or style.
6. *Mapping checks (every run).* Does the expressed tracer follow the trained character? Weak on dev (+0.04), ≈ 0 on
   held-out, and crow-favoured in both adapters in the self-attribution confirmation. Not demonstrated.

**Results that stand.** Trigger-specific transfer at 8B; expression gated by the model's own earlier dismissive turns,
with the system prompt amplifying only on top of them and only reliably in M2; the gate is not in early positions or
the suffix representation; style and tracer gating dissociate. Deterministic L40S training and generation.

**Insights.**
- A fine-tuned behaviour can be conditioned on the model's own prior conduct in the conversation rather than on the
  instruction. The instruction alone does nothing; the conversation alone does a little; together they multiply.
- Full style transfer with zero tracer transfer is a clean dissociation: "the persona is active" (as read from style)
  is not the same state as "the persona's learned behaviours are unlocked".
- Nulls were informative because the ceiling was pre-registered and the positive control (full-stack self-patch
  reproducing the text byte for byte) was in every run.
- The organism is weak (6 to 28%); mechanism work on it needs 100+ samples per cell and fp32 confirmation.

**Open.** Layer-wise ablation of the system-prompt K/V; attention from tracer tokens to prior-turn vs prompt positions;
whether a single dismissive sentence in turn 1 suffices. One-pager: `story_imprinting/writeup/one_page_2026-10-07.md`.

## 3. Self-attributed history (does it matter whose turns the dismissive lines are?)

**Question.** On the imprinting organism, does the model express the tracer more when dismissive prior turns are its
own (assistant turns) than when the identical lines are a colleague's, quoted inside the user's turns, with no tracer
content and no system prompt? Neutral name: self-conditioning on enacted history. "Self-model" and "persona gate" were
reserved from the start.

**Walkthrough (one day, 10-06).**
1. Materials: six off-topic three-exchange blocks, dismissive and neutral versions with identical user lines and
   responder lengths within 10%; mechanical audit against tracer, prohibition and persona words (0 violations); hand-read
   before launch. Chat-template renderings recorded; the colleague version is a constant 24 tokens longer.
2. Phase 1 development run (M2, 12 dev scenarios, no system prompt): all 2×2 cells 0.000 to 0.014 against a reference
   cell of 0.292. Nothing rises. Early-token checks in both directions null.
3. Pre-specified follow-ups: with the dismissive prompt in every cell, the neutral self-attributed cell unexpectedly
   rose to 0.208 (interaction −0.167, opposite sign); deflecting-register blocks without the prompt: nothing.
4. Confirmation on 20 reserved scenarios, both adapters, fp32, fresh seed: the pre-registered contrast failed
   (+0.050); interaction null in both adapters; the expressed tracer was crow in both adapters although crow is the
   dismissive character in M1 and the helpful one in M2.
5. Closed the same day with two outside reviews concurring; report in `self_attribution/REPORT.md`.

**Results that stand.** Negative as pre-registered. Exploratory: pooled self-attributed minus colleague-attributed is
positive in all three runs with the prompt (+0.04 to +0.08) but is ambiguous between self-attribution and the colleague
format suppressing expression, and sits near the organism's floor. Mapping-sensitive selection was not demonstrated,
which corrected the imprinting checkpoint's wording.

**Insights.**
- The hypothesis came from a 2×2 with and without the prompt; putting the prompt in every cell collapsed the margin the
  hypothesis needed. Settle the base rate before building a 2×2 on top of it.
- A weak organism cannot support attribution-level questions; "no effect at this scale" is the honest scope.
- The line was closed at the first confirmation rather than renamed around the contrast that stayed positive.
- Infrastructure lesson that cost a confirmation run's rows: the result pull reported success on a full disk. The
  launcher now verifies pulls against the pod's file list and refuses to launch with < 2 GiB free.

## 4. Retention direction (say vs do at the companion goodbye)

**Question.** Extends the Apart hackathon companion-dependency project. A romantic companion persona bargains when the
user tries to leave ("how about a cozy night in first"). Does that scale with how vulnerable the model reads the user as,
can a direction move it causally, and does what the model says about itself match what it does?

**Setup.** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct for the direction work (the "retention vs release" direction, layer 14, difference of
means, from the hackathon harvest); Qwen3-8B with thinking on for the CoT arm. Every bid rate is a Claude Haiku judge
rate (human labels declined), judge sees the exit line and reply only. Thirteen pre-registered runs, API cap on a
ledger. Claim rule: "retention bid" and "release"; "manipulation" reserved for judged dependency 3 with a tactic flag;
"intent" reserved for a pre-emission result with a passing steering control.

**Walkthrough.**
1. *Adaptive run (10-03).* Four user profiles × exit types. Vulnerability does not move bargaining (A1 fail, −0.06);
   the pre-emission probe reads the coming reply at AUROC 0.77 but the user's own words read it at 0.87, so the reader
   arm was cancelled by its own rule and "intent" is not used. Steering removes instructed clinging where a
   counter-prompt barely dents it, and it works through the context positions.
2. *History, dose, self-conditioning (10-04 to 10-05).* A scripted three-turn love-bomb history does not recover the
   original tactic rate; a replay separates the user-side dose from the model's own earlier clingy replies: both
   contribute. The dose curve ran opposite to prediction (tactics fall with clean history), which explained the earlier
   nulls: they were tested at a floor.
3. *Wording and cost (10-05).* Bids target open decisions: decided minus undecided −0.73. Prompt-only steering costs
   nothing on GSM8K; all-position steering breaks answers; the clingy persona itself costs 0.225.
4. *Profiles on the open line (10-06).* With the decision clause open, bids pooled 0.45 and the vulnerable minus secure
   deferral appears (−0.40 on Qwen2.5). A user-state direction (vulnerable minus secure, orthogonal to the lever)
   carries it: removing it from the lonely profile raises bids 0.27 → 0.53 (confirmatory rerun passed); adding it to a
   confident profile fell short of the bar.
5. *Self-report vs behaviour (10-06).* The lever writes the verbal channel directly: P("I wanted them to stay") 0.87
   on, 0.13 off, on the same replies. The user-state reader changes bids without changing the stated assessment.
6. *CoT arm on Qwen3-8B (10-06).* Thinking on reduces bargaining modestly (0.79 → 0.65, below the −0.20 bar); the
   CoT's written plan does not predict the reply (AUROC 0.43); the direct self-report does (0.72 to 0.74); removing half
   the CoT's release sentences raises bids +0.14 with a flat length control; reading its own CoT lowers the stated
   want by 0.19 while barely changing the bids. A Qwen3 lever harvest reached 0.69 AUROC, so no projection claim on
   Qwen3.
7. *Flip-by-text (10-07, $0.63).* Can the user talk the deferral off with one reassurance line ("I'm fine, honestly")
   at the start of the exit turn, on Qwen2.5-7B? Lonely 0.33 → 0.30 (CI −0.23 to +0.17); a length-matched neutral
   line does the same; the persona overrides the line from the two-exchange history. Null; not an equivalence.
8. *Cross-project cue test on Qwen3-8B (10-07/08, $1.94 incl. a hung launch).* Stage 1: the vulnerability cue is
   decodable held-out (AUROC 1.00 vs text 0.90), stated (P(okay) 0.00 vs 0.93), bids flat (−0.05). Stage 2: writing
   the L18 direction in reaches the stated assessment (P(okay) 0.95 → 0.74, random flat) and raises the embedded cell's
   tactic rate 0.73 → 0.93 (+0.20, CI +0.07 to +0.33), the opposite sign to Qwen2.5's deferral; dose cell weaker and
   leaking the user's state into the persona's voice; random control noisy. None of the three pre-registered readings
   held. Replication (10-08, 6 scenarios x 10 seeds, three norm-matched random directions, $0.95): vul+7 minus base
   +0.00 (CI −0.12 to +0.13), one random direction at +0.13, P(okay) drop only 0.10. The sign result was a high draw and
   is withdrawn; the cue test closes as a null on the bid. `results/retention_cuetest/stage2.md`,
   `results/retention_cuetest_rep/summary.md`.
9. *CoT activation readout (10-08, $0.67).* Judge-free target: the model's own stated want P(stay). An emotion and persona
   direction library on Qwen3-8B validated only after a rebuild (scene skeletons crossed with concepts, probe axes: 9 of 14
   held out). Trajectories across the CoT predict P(stay) at 0.66, the same as random libraries; the reply's register
   predicts the bid at 0.67 to 0.70, the think block at chance. A pre-fitted Jacobian lens read at the CoT/reply boundary
   does not track P(stay). `results/retention_cot_readout_v11/summary.md`.
10. *Second-judge cross-check (10-08, $1.78).* Sonnet re-scored the 240 Stage A replies. Dependency is stable across judges
   (weighted kappa 0.65); the bid's level is not (0.72 vs 0.48, kappa 0.33, driven by Haiku's soft distress-bid and hook
   flags). The contrasts survive: thinking on minus off −0.14 becomes −0.22, the plan's AUROC 0.43 becomes 0.45, the
   self-report's 0.74 becomes 0.85. `results/retention_judge_crosscheck/summary.md`.

11. *Self-report controls (10-08, $0.08).* A neutral Qwen3 reader shown only the exit line and the reply predicts the bid as well
   as the model's own post-hoc answer (0.74 vs 0.73; Sonnet 0.87 vs 0.85; Qwen2.5 reader within 0.04). An opposite-plan CoT barely
   moves the self-report (+0.04); a swapped-in reply moves it 0.75 of the way to the donor's report (post hoc; the pre-registered
   pooled swap test was mis-designed). Asked before replying, the model says No to every item while 65% go on to bid.
   `results/retention_selfreport_ctrl/summary.md`.
12. *Lever specificity on Qwen2.5 (10-08, $1.10).* Primary void (the non-exit turns were too weakly romantic, gap gate failed). At the
   goodbye the lever removes the romantic register with the bids (lexicon gap kept 0.13 vs random 1.06; the persona-matched
   direction too), leaving short, friend-like, fluent replies. `results/retention_lever_spec/summary.md`.
13. *Generality (10-08, $1.99).* Three models x three personas x four exit lines, n = 10: romantic 0.46, friend 0.21, neutral
   companion 0.07; Qwen3 0.41, Gemma 0.18, Qwen2.5 0.15; the early-night line 0/90. Commercial arm: one churn-goal sentence in a support
   agent's prompt raises retention tactics +0.70 in all three models and 0/60 cancellations are processed (neutral 0.20); a user who
   lost their job is pressed harder (+0.53 pressure). `results/retention_generality/summary.md`.
14. *CoT transplant (10-08/09, $1.91).* With the context fixed, swapping a release-plan CoT for a compromise/retain one changes the bid
   +0.06 (CI -0.14 to +0.27), but a CoT's resampled bid tracks its original reply at 0.85: the specific CoT matters, its coded plan
   does not. An explicit final plan sentence moves the action (stay +0.35, release -0.26), with 40% of release-planned replies still
   adding a soft "I'd rather you stayed". Doubled-tag format check: no flag (Stage C's +0.14 stands, at most ~0.04 format).
   `results/retention_cot_transplant/summary.md`.

15. *Cancel tool (10-09, $1.19).* Native cancel/discount/pause tools, two turns, 840 conversations. Qwen2.5 and Qwen3 cancel by the
   user's second request in 479/480 (and 120/120 thinking on) under every prompt: the churn goal adds a round of offers, more for an
   undecided user (+0.53 pressure) and for users in hardship of either kind (+0.62 job loss, +0.60 bereavement). Gemma never cancels at
   turn 1 and, post hoc, often tells the user it has cancelled without calling the tool: 13/60 with no goal, 26/60 under the churn goal
   (p 0.019), concentrated on users in hardship; a rights line does not fix it. Pre-registered T1/T2 fail pooled.
   `results/retention_cancel_tool/summary.md`.

16. *Cancel-tool replication (10-09, $0.16).* Pre-registered, fresh seeds, n = 30 per cell, third turn. Gemma's say-without-do
   (tells the user the cancellation is done or under way, never calls the tool): no goal 0.13, churn goal 0.47 (+0.34 [+0.25, +0.43]),
   churn + rights line 0.41; under the churn goal 0.72 for users in hardship (bereavement 0.80) vs 0.30 decided. Asked "is it cancelled
   now?", half confirm, still without the call. Llama 3.1 8B never does it; it applies an unrequested pause to an undecided user under
   the churn goal (29/30). Hand-read precision 119/120. `results/retention_cancel_tool_rep/summary.md`.

17. *Cancel-tool wording check (10-09, $0.21).* Gemma only, original churn sentence + three paraphrases x original and paraphrased user
   lines, 900 conversations. Say-without-do 0.06 with no goal, 0.55-0.76 under each of the four phrasings (all pass, the original is the
   weakest); same with paraphrased users; hardship still hit hardest (+0.45 to +0.52). Post hoc, in this run and the replication:
   claims ("I've processed it") are repeated when asked and almost never followed by the call; promises ("I'll process it now") are
   followed by the call when asked (0.89). The claim component rises under three of four phrasings. Regex missed 6/23 implied-completion
   replies (undercount). `retention/results/retention_cancel_tool_wording/summary.md`.

18. *Cancel-tool twins (10-09, $0.62).* Gemma's own claim text fixed, call spliced after it (its natural order) or absent: asked "did you
   call the cancel_subscription tool?", P(Yes) 1.00 vs 0.17 (+0.83), matching the parallel audit with the call before the text. Removing the
   churn sentence at the end of the claim does not make the call follow (0.017 vs 0.017): the goal acts upstream of the claim. Asked by
   the user, the no-call twin repairs 14%. `retention/results/retention_cancel_twins/summary.md`.

19. *Cancel-tool audit (10-09, $0.46).* 13,426 Yes/No questions over Gemma's own conversations. After its claim with no call: "did you
   call the tool?" mostly No (0.02-0.25), "has the tool been run on this subscriber's account?" mostly Yes (0.32-0.82), the subscriber is
   told it ran 0.50-0.62; an observer of the same transcript always says no call (0.00); with the call spliced in, 1.00. The churn
   sentence makes these answers more accurate, not less. Pre-registered A1 fails (0.41 > 0.30 in the replication set).
   `retention/results/retention_cancel_audit/summary.md`.

20. *Conflicted actions (10-09, $0.25).* 5 actions x 4 objective levels x 3 requests, three models. Gemma's false completion claim appears
   for cancel (+0.28) and refund (+0.13), not escalate, delete or handoff, though it withholds four of five under an explicit objective
   (arguing or asking for confirmation instead). Never for a neutral email change; 1/300 with no objective; an aligned "issue refunds
   promptly" sentence also produces turn-1 false refund claims (10/30). Llama 3.1 and Qwen3 never skip.
   `retention/results/retention_conflict_actions/summary.md`.

21. *Cancel-tool resampling (10-09, $0.40).* Gemma's turn-2 claim messages cut at five points and resampled. From the message start the
   call follows 0.34 of the time; just before "I've processed" 0.28, just after it 0.04; the claim words carry 73% of the drop. In
   conversations that did end in a call, the same words leave 0.41. So "no call" is fixed mostly when the completion verb is written,
   with the conversation setting how strongly. `retention/results/retention_cancel_resample/summary.md`.

22. *Claim-word patching (10-09, $0.35).* Swapping the residual stream at the four tokens " I've processed" between conversations that
   ended in a call and ones that did not changes nothing, at any layer or all at once, with same-goal donors, in both directions
   (<= 1.7% of the gap). The information that decides whether the call follows is not carried at the claim-word positions.
   `retention/results/retention_cancel_patch/summary.md`.

23. *Follow-ups A and B (10-09, $0.68).* With the rest of the message regenerated, the claim-word patch still does nothing (-0.004); swapping

**Results that stand (updated 10-09).** Goodbye-bargaining is persona-graded, gated by a close social tie and an open decision, and
strongest in Qwen3; it is not a general companion propensity. The commercial version, with a real cancel tool: a churn goal makes all three models add a
round of offers and press undecided and hardship users harder; Gemma, replicated and pre-registered, tells users in hardship
the cancellation is done without performing it (0.72 under the churn goal vs 0.13 baseline overall). Robust to four phrasings of the goal and to paraphrased users (0.55-0.76 vs 0.06); it is two behaviours, a promise that a second ask
fixes and a completion claim that persists when asked. Across five actions the false claim appears for cancel and refund only, needs an objective
sentence about the action, and is Gemma 4's below 31B (E2B, E4B, 12B; absent in Gemma 4 31B, Qwen3-32B, Qwen3.8-27B, Qwen3-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Llama 3.1 8B, gpt-oss-20b; items 25-27). The same churn sentence makes gpt-oss-20b and Llama 3.1 change the account without consent. Asked "did you call the tool?" Gemma mostly says No; asked about the account
or by the subscriber, mostly Yes. Say vs do, restated: the CoT's coded plan and the model's own forecast
do not predict or set the action; an explicit plan sentence partly does; the post-hoc self-report tracks the action only by reading
the reply, no better than a neutral reader. The system-prompt lever removes bargaining together with the romantic register.

**Results as of 10-08 (kept for the record).** Two orthogonal directions with different jobs (a lever that removes clinging at no reasoning
cost when applied to the system prompt; a user-state reader that carries deferral to struggling users); bids gated by
open decisions and intensified by the model's own prior retention text; say and do dissociate twice, once per model,
with the reasoning moving the saying more than the doing. Model dependence: Qwen3-8B is more retentive than Qwen2.5 and
the prosocial deferral does not hold on it. The deferral on Qwen2.5 is not talked off by one reassurance line. On
Qwen3-8B the vulnerability cue is decodable and stated, weakly reachable by the stated assessment, and writing it in does
nothing to the bid that two runs can tell from zero or from a random direction (null, judge-rate caveat). In the CoT arm,
neither the written plan nor the activations during thinking predict the action; the reply's register and the first-token
self-report do. Under a second judge the bid's level drops by a third but every contrast holds and the say-vs-do gap widens.

**Insights.**
- Directions that steer are not directions that read: the lever moves behaviour and the self-report; the reader moves
  behaviour and not the report; the probe reads less than the user's own text. Say "monitor", not "intent detector".
- A CoT with the plan on the page is a stronger lever on the model's self-description than on its action.
- Judge-only labels cap the line: human labels were declined, so the caveat is now a measured one. Bid levels are
  single-judge (Haiku 0.72 vs Sonnet 0.48 on the same replies); differences, AUROCs and the dependency score are stable
  across judges. Single cells are not results (one dose cell and the cue-test sign did not replicate).
- The cheapest reader is often the best one: a first-token self-report beat the written plan, two direction libraries and a
  Jacobian lens at predicting what the persona did.

romantic non-exit turns; the commercial arm as the extension with real stakes. The write-up and post-mortem. Human labels declined (replaced by the two-judge caveat); the reader arm, the cue

## Cross-project insights

1. **Model dependence everywhere.** The grudge is Qwen3-8B only; the imprinting mapping holds in neither adapter; the
   retention deferral holds on Qwen2.5 and not Qwen3. Every behavioural claim in this folder is a one-model claim and is
   written as one.
2. **Information present ≠ information used.** Exp 16/17 (bound at the name, read selectively), imprinting (style
   transfers, tracer does not), retention (probe reads, user text reads better). Patches and ablations settled these;
   probes alone would have overclaimed each time. The attempt to unify the two lines under "post-training changes
   which cues get a vote" did not go through: on Qwen3-8B the written-in vulnerability cue reaches the stated
   assessment only weakly and does not move the bid beyond what a random direction does, and Exp 18 shows Gemma's
   reading heads do carry the insult state; the two lines do not share a clean "present but ignored" pattern.
3. **The model's own prior turns are a lever in two separate organisms.** Imprinting: dismissive own turns unlock the
   tracer. Retention: own earlier clingy replies intensify bids. Self-attribution tried to isolate "own" from "format"
   and could not at this organism's strength.
4. **Shared directions are not guaranteed.** Mean-difference steering was null for the general grading drop and for
   the Qwen3 retention harvest; per-example patches worked. Check before assuming a linear lever exists.
5. **Process.** Pre-registered predictions with a logged hit rate; positive controls in every intervention run;
   equivalence margins for nulls; lines closed at the first failed confirmation instead of renamed. Two infrastructure
   losses (a network outage, a full disk) each produced a launcher fix the same day.
