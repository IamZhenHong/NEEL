## Priority 3 — Rigour (this is what wins sprints, and it's your strength)

- **Manually verify outputs.** Don't let an LLM (or Claude Code) hand you a result you
  didn't check. Pull random samples and read them yourself. (Both Olivia and Mira caught
  false results this way — it's the discipline Nanda prizes most.)
- **Use compute generously for validation.** You're reimbursed; top-ups available if you ask
  early. Don't run 10 samples to save money when 50–100 gives you a real answer. Don't let
  cost bottleneck accuracy or progress.
- **Controls, always.** Random-direction controls, matched baselines, orthogonalise
  confounds. "Similar vectors are similar" and "the ablation just degraded the model" are
  the failure modes to pre-empt.
- **Red-team your own results.** Assume a surprising result is false until you've ruled out
  the boring explanation (cache bug, truncated budget, saturated judge, matcher bug).
- **Stay pragmatic (Nanda's frame):** simple methods first (diff-of-means beats SAEs when it
  works), a real safety North Star, a checkable proxy task. Don't nerd-snipe into
  ungroundable/philosophical territory.
